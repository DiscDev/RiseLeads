Field guide / Terms & conditions

Read the terms,
not just the headline.

A practical way to assess account rules, bonus restrictions and withdrawal conditions before making a decision.

Original educational guide · Public regulator references · Not a casino recommendation

A promotional headline describes a possibility. The contract describes the restrictions. Read both, and treat any gap between them as a question to resolve, not a reason to hurry.

This guide offers a reading method, not a test of any casino. It uses public guidance from the Gambling Commission in Great Britain as a concrete reference. That guidance does not establish the rules in another country, your legal eligibility or the quality of a particular operator. If the local position is unclear, do not assume an English-language site or familiar currency means you may use it.

01. Start with the jurisdiction, not the logo

Identify the entity named in the contract, the exact website domain and the regulator the operator claims. In Great Britain, the Gambling Commission advises checking the licence and whether its activities and domain names match the public register. Follow the register entry rather than treating a licence image in a footer as a completed check. A familiar trading name can be used by a different legal entity or for a different market.

The Commission’s public register explains its fields, including licence status, activities, trading names and domain names. It also notes that some information is supplied by the business. A register match is a specific observation at a particular time, not a guarantee of future conduct, universal authorisation or protection from losses.

02. Find the whole contract

Locate general account terms, product rules, promotional terms, payment rules, privacy information and complaints procedures. Check which legal entity each document names, whether it applies to your intended market and whether a version or effective date is shown. If two pages appear to conflict, ask for clarification in writing before relying on the more attractive interpretation.

The Commission’s opening-accounts guidance identifies information gambling businesses must provide in its jurisdiction, including licensed status, account fees, money protection, offers and terms. Use that as a starting map, not as proof that any website has met those duties. A missing or location-restricted document remains inaccessible evidence. Do not use a cached snippet as though it were the current agreement.

03. Separate your money from bonus funds

Work out which balance is your deposit and which is promotional credit. Ask whether accepting a bonus changes the treatment of your own money, how funds are consumed during play and what happens if you decline or cancel an offer. Read the offer’s eligibility, opt-in requirements, expiry and excluded activities together. “Free” in a headline is not a substitute for those conditions.

When a wagering requirement exists, identify the base it applies to, the qualifying games, contribution rules, maximum permitted bet and deadline. A multiplier without a defined base is incomplete information. Do not infer a required turnover from the headline alone, and do not treat a theoretical turnover calculation as the expected cost of completing an offer. Gambling outcomes are uncertain and completing a requirement can still involve losing your money.

The Commission’s free-offers guidance says businesses should distinguish personal funds from restricted bonus funds and make restrictions clear. It also describes keeping remaining deposits and winnings earned from deposit play, within its regulatory context. That is regulator guidance, not our confirmation of an individual offer or legal advice for a dispute.

04. Read withdrawal and identity rules together

Check how a withdrawal is requested, the stated processing stages, possible limits, fees, available payment methods and any account-verification requirements. Distinguish operator processing time from the time a payment provider may take. A quoted estimate is not a tested withdrawal speed. If a rule says “business days,” identify whether the document explains the relevant calendar and exceptions.

Look for clear explanations of identity checks and the circumstances in which additional information may be requested. Do not send identity documents or banking information to a review publisher, an unverified support address or this website. The presence of a verification process does not establish that the operator is legitimate, and the absence of a public detail does not prove that no check will occur.

Also examine account closure, inactivity charges and what happens to a balance if an account is restricted. If a withdrawal rule is vague or conflicts with the promotion, pause. A customer-service explanation can clarify the operator’s position, but it does not replace the written terms or an independent assessment of your rights.

05. Find the route for problems before there is a problem

Locate the complaints contact, the steps for escalating a complaint and any named dispute-resolution body. Check that the route actually relates to the contracting entity and the applicable market. The existence of a complaints page is not evidence that disputes are resolved well. Likewise, one review site showing no complaints does not establish that no complaints exist.

Review the available safer-gambling information and account tools without assuming they have been independently tested. Limits, time-outs and self-exclusion are distinct mechanisms with different effects. If you are already concerned about your gambling, seeking independent support is more important than finding a more attractive contract. See our responsible-gambling page for a Great Britain support resource and location caveats.

06. Keep a useful record, not a false verdict

Record the document URL, access date, relevant wording, market scope and unresolved questions. Preserve a copy for your own reference where appropriate. Do not label a fact “verified” merely because two directories repeat it. Prefer a current primary record for a legal claim and state when that record could not be accessed.

A simple checklist can have three honest outcomes: located and understood, unclear and awaiting clarification, or not accessible. None is a numerical safety rating. If a material condition remains unknown, declining to proceed is a reasonable decision. You do not need to complete a deposit, claim a promotion or test a withdrawal to justify walking away.
